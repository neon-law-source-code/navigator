[On July 9, 2025, Andreessen Horowitz reincorporated its management company, AH Capital Management, from Delaware to
Nevada](https://a16z.com/were-leaving-delaware-and-we-think-you-should-consider-leaving-too/) and published its reasons.
They believe that Delaware's Court of Chancery "injected an unprecedented level of subjectivity into judicial
decisions." It points to rulings that second-guessed director independence on founder compensation, expanded the "entire
fairness" exception until it swallowed the business judgment rule, and, in one case later reversed, refused to let a
company reincorporate elsewhere at all. The result, in a16z's view, is a forum that "can at times appear biased against
technology startup founders" and imposes litigation costs a startup cannot carry. Nevada answers each point by statute
rather than by judicial discretion: the business judgment rule is codified, directors and officers face monetary
liability only for "intentional misconduct, fraud or a knowing violation of law," books-and-records demands are limited
to holders of fifteen percent or more, and the legislature has authorized jury-trial waivers and a dedicated business
court. Dropbox, TripAdvisor, and Tesla made the same move; a16z's point in going public is that the market penalty
investors once feared for a non-Delaware charter "may be overblown."
